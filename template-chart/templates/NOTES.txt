CHANGING THE SECRET DOES NOT RESTART ANYTHING HERE
  Neither editing the values inside the 1Password item nor repointing
  secrets.xxxSecretRef at a different item rolls the pods: this chart stamps
  checksum/config over the ConfigMap, but no checksum/secret-ref over the
  rendered OnePasswordItem. Helm never sees a secret's content, so the content
  case cannot be closed at all — but the ref case can, and a chart that needs
  it adds the second annotation (README, "Config changes must restart the
  workload"). Until then, after any secret change:

    kubectl -n {{ .Release.Namespace }} rollout restart deployment/{{ .Release.Name }}-{{ .Chart.Name }}-deployment

CONFIGURATION
  {{ .Release.Name }}-{{ .Chart.Name }}-configmap is mounted into the workload, and the pod template
  carries checksum/config over it — so a changed ConfigMap really does roll the
  pods. That annotation is not decoration: a subPath mount is never refreshed
  by the kubelet, and most apps read their config only at start, so without it
  a config change would apply to nothing while everything stayed green.

  The hash is taken over the rendered ConfigMap, not over a timestamp: an
  unchanged config yields an identical hash and the upgrade stays a no-op.

  (storage.storageClass is unset){{ end }}.

  helm uninstall does NOT delete this PVC, which is deliberate — the data
  survives a reinstall of the same release name. It also means a release
  removed and recreated under a DIFFERENT name starts on an empty volume while
  the old one keeps occupying space.

WHAT A GREEN POD PROVES
  All three probes are HTTP GETs against "/" on the port named http. They
  prove that something answers on that port — not that the application is
  functional, and not that anything behind the Ingress works, because probes
  go straight to the pod and never pass through it.

  When you copy this chart, point the probes at a real health endpoint and
  carry the probe's CONTEXT into the fallback defaults in _helpers.tpl, not
  just its timings: a default that loses a port name, a Host header or an exec
  command leaves the workload with a probe that cannot succeed.

THIS CHART IS A SCAFFOLD — IT IS NOT MEANT TO RUN
  If you are reading this from an actual install of template-chart, three
  things will keep it from becoming Ready, all of them on purpose:

  - The image is the placeholder "ghcr.io/ORG/IMAGE:{{ .Chart.AppVersion }}" and does not
    exist, so the pods stay in ImagePullBackOff. This is also why the chart
    sits in ci/excluded-charts.txt and is never published.
  - The chart renders BOTH a Deployment and a StatefulSet, as the two shapes
    to choose between. Delete the one your app does not need; the Service
    selects the Deployment's pods, so a stateful app has to move that selector
    along with it.
  - The env block in values.yaml demonstrates the three supported forms
    (value, secretKeyRef, configMapKeyRef) — it is an example, not a working
    configuration. Note in particular that ENV_FROM_CONFIGMAP names
    "{{ .Release.Name }}-{{ .Chart.Name }}-config-map" while the ConfigMap this chart creates is
    "{{ .Release.Name }}-{{ .Chart.Name }}-configmap": replace the whole block with your app's real
    variables rather than repairing the example.
